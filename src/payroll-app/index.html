Timesheet & computation

Read-only. Every change comes in through the payroll template: Download template (.xlsx), fill it in, Import payroll files. The table then shows each person's figures (daily/hourly rate, OT, SSS, PhilHealth, HDMF, withholding tax and net pay). Overtime is split by the three rates in the process document — 1.25× regular workday, 1.69× rest day or special non-working, 2.60× regular holiday. ND hrs are hours worked 10:00PM–6:00AM, paid at hourly × 10% (auto-suggested as OT hours beyond the first 3, editable). Transp. allow. is entered directly as an amount. De minimis is not entered at all — it is computed every cutoff as the standard ₱1,500, prorated by working days when someone's start date falls inside the cutoff. Both are non-taxable. Managers are exempt from OT, night differential and holiday premium.

Other expenses, allowances & deductions

Add recurring items like Maxicare HMO, rice allowance, company loans or MP2. Choose whether the item hits every payout, only the 1st–15th cutoff, or only the 16th–EOM cutoff.

Loans

Enter a loan once and it amortises itself — the installment number and remaining balance are derived by counting payouts from the start date, so correcting a start date reflows the whole schedule. Deduction stops automatically when the balance reaches zero, and a final short installment is truncated to whatever is left.

13th month pay & annualised tax

13th month is total basic salary earned in the year divided by 12 — absences reduce it, paid leave does not. The adjustment compares tax actually withheld across the year against the tax due on the annualised income: a positive figure is refundable to the employee, a negative one is still collectible.

Trinity payroll (PHP)

Paid every 10th (cutoff 16th–end of month) and 25th (cutoff 1st–15th). Everything here saves for all HR admins.

New hire

  1. Employees tab: re-import the latest 201 File (start date, work email, BPI account, government numbers), or + Add employee.
  2. Give them a monthly rate: Import salaries, or type it in. Without one they are left off the cutoff and a yellow warning names them.
  3. Check they have a TrinityHR login (User Management) with the same work email, or their payslip cannot be published to them.
  4. Starting mid-cutoff, their basic pay and de minimis are prorated on their own; a blue note says Started inside this cutoff. Basic = monthly ÷ 2 − daily rate × working days before the start date, where daily rate = monthly × 12 ÷ 261 (e.g. 24,000 starting Mon Feb 23 in Feb 16–28: 12,000 − 5 × 1,103.45 = 6,482.76). A last day inside a cutoff works the same way, for the days after it.
  5. Worked somewhere else earlier the same year? Get the 2316 from their previous employer and enter it on the BIR 2316 tab (click their name → Previous employer: taxable compensation, tax withheld, TIN, name, address). It goes into their year-end tax and final pay; without it their annual tax is too low and their refund too high. If there was none, tick No previous employer this year. New hires without either are flagged previous employer?.

Each cutoff

  1. Payroll Run: choose the pay period and year.
  2. Download template (.xlsx) — always a fresh one.
  3. Fill in the cream columns: OT and ND hours; quarterly bonus and its details (";" between items, shown on the payslip); travel allowance and deductions; absences, late, undertime; Tendo, HMO, SSS loan, Class pass; other deduction; remarks.
  4. Import payroll files with it. The Payroll Run table is read-only — to change anything, fix the template and import it again.
  5. Check the cards (gross, contributions, tax, net pay, cost to company), that nobody has a negative net pay, and a few people with the Payslip button on their row.

Payslips

  1. Payslip tab → Trinity payroll: choose the cutoff, Prepare payslips.
  2. Preview a few people, then Publish to employees. They see it under My Workspace → Payslips. Preparing again replaces the cutoff's payslips.

BPI bank file

  1. Once: the BPI funding account in Settings & Rules → Company.
  2. Payroll Run → BPI bank file (.xls). Everyone with net pay is in it, their name in column H.
  3. Read the popup: people not on BPI (e.g. GCash), account numbers that are not 10 digits, no bank on file, negative net pay (not in the file). Pay those separately.
  4. Upload in BPI BizLink as "Pay BPI Accounts – Lumpsum" and check the total. Export CSV keeps a record of the cutoff.

Salary increase

  1. In the cutoff's payroll template, fill in NEW RATE (the new monthly rate, e.g. 45,000) and NEW RATE EFFECTIVE DATE for that person, then Import payroll files. The import lists every salary change it applied.
  2. Effective inside the cutoff: it is split by working days (old rate before, new rate from the date; the note says e.g. in force · 6/11 days). Effective later: it shows greyed (takes effect …) until then. Contributions and tax follow the new rate.
  3. From the next cutoff the increase becomes their monthly rate on its own and the adjustment clears; a note says so.
  4. No mid-cutoff split needed: change the monthly rate on the Employees tab, or several at once with Download salary sheet → Import salaries. A payroll file with a different EMPLOYEE RATE also updates it.
  5. Backdated (effective before the cutoff you are on): the new rate is in force from this cutoff, and the back pay since the effective date is worked out and added here as "Salary adjustment — back pay (Sep 15 – Oct 15)" — taxable, counted toward the 13th month. Per earlier cutoff: (new − old) ÷ 2 × working days at the new rate ÷ working days in it. The cell shows it, with remove if it should not be paid.

Resignee

  1. Set their last day of work on the Employees tab (or the next 201 File import brings it).
  2. In their last cutoff, basic pay and de minimis are prorated to that day; a blue note says Last day of work inside this cutoff.
  3. From the next cutoff they are off the payroll on their own. Their past payslips stay.

Final pay

  1. Final Pay tab: everyone with a last day is listed.
  2. Choose Final pay in — the cutoff with their last day, or a later one. That cutoff adds: the 13th month not yet paid (pro rata); unused leave (the year's credits × months worked ÷ 12, less leave taken, at the daily rate; type days to override; tax-free up to the days in Settings); the year's tax adjustment (refund or tax due); any loan balance; clearance deductions you add.
  3. Untick parts that do not apply; write a note if needed. Check Net pay that cutoff. A cutoff after the last day pays only the final pay (no basic, no contributions).
  4. Run that cutoff as usual — payslip, BPI file. The final pay is part of them.
  5. Final pays done in Excel ("Final Pay" tabs): Record final pay from payroll files marks them paid.
  6. At year-end, anyone given final pay is skipped for the 13th month and tax refund.

Year-end tax: how the annual figures are worked out

The 13th Month & Year-End tab. Each cutoff withholds tax with the semi-monthly table — an estimate. At year-end the annual table settles the real tax for the year (the BIR year-end adjustment, "annualization"), and the difference is refunded or collected.

The columns, with an example (De La Paz, Mara)

  1. YTD taxable income — every cutoff paid this year, its NET TAXABLE AMOUNT added up (taxable pay after SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG, less de minimis and other non-taxable pay). Mara: 1,629,895.17.
  2. Taxable — the part of the 13th month, year-end bonus, quarterly bonus and ex milestones (anniversary bonus) above the ₱90,000 tax-free ceiling (BIR 2316 item 48). Transport and de minimis are outside the ceiling (item 35). Mara: 154,066.67 − 90,000 = 64,066.67.
  3. Annual taxable — YTD taxable income + Taxable − the quarterly bonus. Each cutoff withholds on the quarterly bonus in full, so it is taken back out of YTD here and only what the four pass the ceiling by is taxed. It is income, not a sum of taxes. Mara: 1,629,895.17 + 64,066.67 = 1,693,961.84.
  4. Annual tax due — the BIR annual table on annual taxable. Mara is over ₱800,000 to ₱2,000,000, so ₱102,500 + 25% of the excess over ₱800,000: 102,500 + 25% × 893,961.84 = 325,990.46.
  5. Tax withheld — the tax deducted each cutoff (semi-monthly table), added up. Mara: 309,972.60.
  6. Refund / (Due) — tax withheld − annual tax due. Positive: refunded to the employee. In brackets: still owed by the employee. Mara: (16,017.86).

The two tables

  1. Semi-monthly (each cutoff's TAX, in the template and Payroll Run): 0% up to 10,417; 15% over 10,417; 20% over 16,667; 25% over 33,333; 30% over 83,333; 35% over 333,333.
  2. Annual (year-end, Settings & Rules): 0% up to 250,000; 15% over 250,000; 20% over 400,000; 25% over 800,000; 30% over 2,000,000; 35% over 8,000,000.
  3. The semi-monthly table is the annual one ÷ 24, so someone with the same taxable pay every cutoff ends the year with withheld ≈ due (a peso or two of rounding).

Why someone owes or gets a refund

  1. Owes: taxable 13th month or benefits over the ₱90,000 ceiling — no tax was withheld on them during the year — or pay that went up and down between cutoffs.
  2. Refund: cutoffs where the semi-monthly table withheld more than the year needs, e.g. after a large one-off taxable payment, or a new hire — each cutoff withholds as if they earned the full year, but the year's income is only from their start date.
  3. New hire with a previous employer this year: their taxable pay there is added to annual taxable, and the tax they withheld counts toward what is due (BIR 2316 items 22 and 25B). Enter it on the BIR 2316 tab.
  4. Tax refund / adjustment posted on (13th Month & Year-End tab) chooses the payout that refunds or collects it. Left on Do not post — report only, nothing is paid or deducted. Anyone given final pay had theirs settled then and is skipped.

The ₱90,000 ceiling, simply

Every employee has ₱90,000 a year tax-free for the 13th month and other benefits (BIR 2316 item 34). Anything above it is taxed (item 48).

What uses the ₱90,000

  1. 13th month
  2. Anniversary bonus (EX MILESTONES)
  3. Quarterly bonus
  4. Year-end non-taxable bonus (₱3,000 for a full year)
  5. Not in it: de minimis, PTO and transport allowance — always tax-free (item 35). Commission, OT, night differential and holiday pay — always taxable (items 45, 50, 51).

Example

  1. 13th month ₱40,000 + anniversary bonus ₱10,000 = ₱50,000 used. ₱40,000 of the ceiling left.
  2. Quarterly bonus ₱60,000: ₱40,000 fills the rest of the ceiling (tax-free), ₱20,000 is taxed.
  3. Total: 40,000 + 10,000 + 60,000 = 110,000 − 90,000 = ₱20,000 taxable. (With the ₱3,000 year-end bonus too: 113,000 − 90,000 = ₱23,000.)

When the tax happens

  1. In the cutoff: the quarterly bonus is taxed in full, as in the payroll files — the year's 13th month and bonuses are not known yet. In the example, tax is withheld on all ₱60,000.
  2. At year-end (13th Month & Year-End tab): the ₱60,000 is taken back out of the year's taxable income and only the ₱20,000 over the ceiling is added. The tax withheld on the other ₱40,000 comes back in the year-end refund.
  3. Anniversary bonus and 13th month are not taxed in the cutoff; if they alone pass ₱90,000, the excess is taxed at year-end (or in the 13th month payout).

BIR 2316 — step by step

  1. Import every cutoff's payroll file for the year (Payroll Run), including the 13th month payout. Commission goes in the COMMISSION column, quarterly bonus in QUARTERLY BONUS TAXABLE, anniversary bonus in EX MILESTONES.
  2. Settings & Rules → Company: check the employer TIN, registered address and ZIP (items 12–14A).
  3. Employees tab (201 File): fix any TIN flagged on the BIR 2316 tab (item 3).
  4. BIR 2316 tab: click each flagged name and fill RDO, registered address, ZIP and birth date (items 5–7).
  5. New hires this year: enter the previous employer's 2316 — TIN, name, address, taxable compensation (item 22) and tax withheld (item 25B) — or tick No previous employer this year.
  6. 13th Month & Year-End tab: check the year-end tax, then choose the payout that posts the refund / (due).
  7. BIR 2316 tab: review the items — 34 (max ₱90,000), 35, 36, 39, 45, 48, 50, 51, 52 and 19–28 — then Export the .xlsx to fill the forms.

Where each item comes from

  1. 19 = 38 + 52 · 20 = 38 · 21 = 52 · 22 previous employer · 23 = 21 + 22 · 24 tax due · 25A present employer, 25B previous · 26 = 25A + 25B · 27 blank · 28 = 26 + 27.
  2. 34 13th month + anniversary + quarterly bonus (max ₱90,000) · 35 de minimis + PTO + transport · 36 employee SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG · 37 blank · 38 = 34 to 37.
  3. 39 basic pay less contributions · 45 commissions · 48 excess over ₱90,000 · 50 OT · 51 night differential and holiday pay · 52 = 39 to 51. Items 29–33, 40–44, 46, 47 and 49 are not applicable.

Computation rules & assumptions

  1. Pay periods. The 25th pays the 1st–15th of the same month. The 10th pays the 16th to the end of the previous month.
  2. Schedule. Monday–Friday, 10:00AM–7:00PM, 1-hour unpaid break, 8 paid hours a day. Saturday and Sunday are rest days.
  3. Overtime. Credited for hours worked after 7:00PM. Regular workday 1.25×; rest day or special non-working day 1.69× (130% base × 130% OT premium); regular holiday 2.60× (200% base × 130% OT premium).
  4. Night differential. Hourly rate × 10% × night hours worked, for hours between 10:00PM and 6:00AM. The 10% is computed on the base hourly rate, per the process document.
  5. Transportation allowance. Entered directly as an amount per cutoff, non-taxable.
  6. De minimis. Computed automatically, never encoded: the standard ₱1,500 per cutoff, non-taxable, prorated by working days when a start date falls inside the cutoff. Someone hired after the cutoff closed gets none. Change the standard amount in Settings.
  7. Contributions. SSS from the 2026 table (Regular EE + MPF EE); PhilHealth 2.5% employee and 2.5% employer; HDMF a flat ₱200 each side. Each is deducted whole on its own payout: SSS on the 25th (pay period 2), PhilHealth and HDMF on the 10th (pay period 1).
  8. Managers. Treated as managerial staff exempt under Art. 82 of the Labor Code — no overtime, night differential, rest day or holiday premium.
  9. Daily rate. Monthly × 12 ÷ 261 working days (Mon–Fri with regular holidays paid). Change the factor above if your company uses 313, 314 or 365.
  10. Contributions. SSS, PhilHealth and HDMF are monthly amounts based on the monthly basic rate, split in half across the two payouts by default.
  11. Tax. Withholding is computed per payout on gross taxable pay less the employee's statutory contributions. Non-taxable allowances (rice, de minimis) are excluded.
  12. Not included. 13th month pay, final pay, leave conversion and the BIR 2316 annualisation are outside this tool's scope.

Check before live use. Statutory rates change by circular. Verify the SSS contribution schedule, the PhilHealth premium rate and the HDMF ceiling against the current issuances, and have the setup reviewed by your accountant before running real payroll.
